1. Purpose of this page ↑ Back to top

This page collects, in one place, the citations behind claims made in a three-part video series (plus several short-form videos) examining public statements made by Alex Baber, a self-described cold case investigator, about his role in the Zodiac Killer and Black Dahlia cases. The videos are the primary content; this page exists so viewers, journalists, and platforms evaluating Baber's claims can trace each factual assertion back to its source without having to scrub through hours of footage.

Every numbered citation[0] in this document links to a specific video, article, or document. Where a claim rests on witness testimony rather than a public record, that is stated explicitly, along with how many independent sources corroborate it. This page does not assert that Baber has been convicted of any crime; where legal or financial disputes are referenced, they are described as disputes, filings, or licensing records — not findings of guilt.

Michael Connelly, a bestselling crime novelist, wrote, produced, and narrates Killer in the Code, a commercial podcast built around Baber's investigation, and brought retired LAPD detectives Mitzi Roberts and Rick Jackson on to publicly endorse Baber's findings.[1] Both have also long worked as Connelly's paid consultants on his Bosch and Ballard novels and their TV adaptations — a financial relationship worth weighing against the independence of their endorsement.[2] Cryptographer Ed Giorgio's cryptanalysis, also featured on the podcast, is described by Connelly himself as validating only the "internal consistency" of a method that already assumed Baber's candidate, Marvin Margolis — not an independent test of Baber's conclusions or Margolis's guilt.[3] No fully independent review of Baber's conclusions has been publicly documented.

3. Claims specific to the current investigation ↑ Back to top

These are the claims examined in Part 3 that relate most directly to Baber's current Zodiac/Black Dahlia investigation. Each is followed, where available, by Baber's response.

Self-contradiction — strongest documented claim

Z340 cipher: "I got verification I was the first one to solve it" vs. "I have never claimed to have solved Z340"

In 2020, Baber posted publicly that he had solved the Z340 cipher "6+ months" before it was solved and announced by an independent team (David Oranchak, Sam Blake, and Jarl Van Eycke) in December 2020, writing "wait until the entire story is out."[4] On a livestream roughly two months before that public solve, Baber described a partial decryption and stated on camera that he had gotten "verification I was the first one to solve it."[5] Years later, in a written message to Oranchak, Baber stated: "I have never claimed to have solved Z340; my focus at the time was limited to the ciphers previously noted which excluded Z13."[6]

These three statements — an archived public post, on-camera livestream footage, and a direct written denial — are all in Baber's own words and are mutually exclusive. This claim does not depend on any third party's account.

Timeline discrepancy

Timing of the Marvin Margolis / "Marvin Merrill" identification

Baber has described identifying Marvin Skipton Margolis (also referred to as "Marvin Merrill") as a suspect only after decoding the Zodiac's "Z13" cipher, and has said he "had no idea" who Marvin Merrill was before that.[7] Research materials reviewed for this series[92][93] — including a 2019 email exchange[8] with a contributor who says he informed Baber of the Margolis/Merrill connection — indicate Baber was promoting Margolis as a suspect as early as 2019.

Baber's responseThe researcher is acknowledged by Baber to have assisted with early Margolis/Lawrence Kane research; Baber states this involvement "has been publicly disclosed and documented" and that the overall investigation "extended well beyond those efforts."[9]
Timeline discrepancy

When Baber says he first contacted his suspect's son

Baber has stated he contacted his suspect's son — whose identity is withheld here — in 2024 under false pretenses before later revealing his true intentions, and has separately said he tracked the son down through genealogy research and social media. Footage reviewed from a 2021 interview shows Baber describing having already secured that individual's cooperation by 2021 or earlier, using the son's and a step-brother's first names.[10] It is not clear why Baber had to use false pretenses in 2024 to secure the son's cooperation when he claimed in 2021 he already had it.

Direct comparison to the primary forensic report

The "Elizabeth" sketch: what the Reis report actually says, vs. how it has been represented

Baber commissioned forensic imaging examiner George Reis, of Imaging Forensics, to analyze a sketch of a topless woman labeled "ELIZABETH" and signed "Marty Merrill '92,"[11] to determine whether writing was present in a shaded area of the drawing. Reis's report, dated January 13, 2026, is now available in full and can be compared directly against Baber's public characterizations of it.[12]

What the report says Reis actually did: he tested the sketch with 45-degree lighting, raking light, back illumination, ultraviolet light, and infrared light — and states plainly that "none of these methods resulted in significant results." The only technique that revealed anything was co-axial illumination,[13] a specialized method using a coated two-way mirror to send light along the camera's optical path — equipment and a technique not available to an ordinary phone camera or consumer app.

What the report says was found: using co-axial lighting, Reis identified marks "consistent with letters" in the area above the "T" in "ELIZABETH" — describing the left-most mark as "appearing to be the letter Z" and the right-most as "the letter C," with a mark between them "that could be another C or a lower-case A," and additional markings in between that he calls "less distinct." The report never states that the word "ZODIAC" was found, confirmed, or read in full — its conclusion is limited to a few individually hedged, partially-legible letter fragments.

The deviation: Baber has publicly described this material as showing the word "ZODIAC" hidden in the sketch, and has at various times attributed the discovery to his own use of a "contrast spectrum" technique. Baber has not corroborated the process he says he personally used to see "ZODIAC" in the sketch before commissioning this report, and the report he cites as validation does not confirm that word was ever there.

4. A pattern predating the current investigation ↑ Back to top

Beyond the claims specific to the Margolis investigation, the series documents a longer pattern of disputed or contradicted statements about Baber's background:

  • Employment as a motor vehicle salesman. Baber has denied a history working as a car salesman, telling one journalist[42] that critics were making up claims to "fit your narrative." Public licensing records identified in Pennsylvania (1997) and Tennessee (2018) show he held motor-vehicle salesman licenses in those states, and a Delaware debt-collection matter connected to that employment resulted in a default judgment and wage-garnishment order.[16] Two different dealership owners were interviewed and claimed Baber engaged in fraudulent activity and theft[41].
    Baber's response: Baber acknowledged working for the "automotive industry" but gave no specifics or explanations[43]. In a YouTube comment, Baber claims one of the dealership owners has a "documented history of fraud allegations" and cites bad reviews on sites such as Yelp, Birdseye, and Glassdoor. He did not provide any examples of the fraud allegations[44].
  • Departure from Austin's Beauty College. The owner of the business, interviewed directly, states Baber was terminated after roughly three months for conduct she describes as untrustworthy, including an allegation of attempted theft from the business; a second, independent named witness corroborates the account of his termination and conduct there. Baber states he was not terminated, disputes any theft allegation, and gave conflicting explanations for ending his employment there: 1) Leaving for a full-time employment opportunity after assisting with grant and military-funded admissions; 2) Leaving due to family health reasons.[17]
  • Military and protective-service background. Baber has referenced special-operations experience and protective-services work for diplomats, including a suggestion that the unit portrayed in Black Hawk Down was his own. Sources who say they contacted Baber's mother report she told them he did not serve; Baber states his mother has confirmed she was never contacted about this and disputes the claim. Neither an affirmative service record nor its absence has been independently confirmed here.[18] A December 23, 2021 post from Baber's own Facebook account pairs a photo of a young man in a military-style dress uniform with a recent photo of Baber, captioned "30 years apart... the reflection has definitely changed" — implying it depicts him. Researchers who have reviewed the image consider it inconclusive at best: the insignia and uniform details are not clearly identifiable to a specific branch or unit, and the image's presentation has raised questions about its authenticity. No forensic image analysis has been performed for this page, and the photo does not resolve the underlying dispute either way — it's noted here only as a data point Baber himself put into the public record.[19]
  • 2007 Zodiac phone call claim. Baber has stated the Zodiac killer personally called him after he posted a cipher solution online in 2007. No corroborating evidence for this call has been produced.[20]
  • Claimed exoneration of William Heirens, via an undisclosed fingerprint match. In a written exchange, Baber stated: "I already have a match. 19 points of identification. This print proves William Heirens was falsely imprisoned and served 65 years for 3 homicides he did not commit."[21] No forensic documentation of this claimed match has been made public. Note: Heirens's original 1946 conviction has independently been the subject of long-standing controversy unrelated to Baber; this entry addresses only whether Baber has substantiated the specific match he says he possesses, not the separate question of Heirens's guilt.
  • "Forensic Linguistics Database." Baber and a former collaborator have described proprietary software said to cross-reference over 1,000 criminal documents to find hidden linguistic links between cases. Baber has at times called the software "hypothetical" and at other times stated he applied it to decode Z13. No independent demonstration of the software has been documented.[22]
  • "Cold case consultant" credentials. Two established cold-case organizations — Cold Case Solutions and Resources, and the Cold Case Foundation — state in writing that they have no record of ever working with Baber, despite his public branding as a cold-case consultant.[23]
  • Sourcing and credit disputes. Multiple individuals — including Black Dahlia researcher Steve Hodel and another Zodiac researcher whose identity is withheld here[92][93] — state Baber used research they contributed without adequate credit. Baber states Hodel's contributions have been "acknowledged" and documented with law enforcement, and that the other researcher's role "has been publicly disclosed."[24]

5. Financial and legal history ↑ Back to top

Sourcing note: Court records were obtained directly from six state court systems (Delaware, Illinois, Maryland, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Tennessee). Every claim below is tied to a specific verifiable docket. Business plaintiffs are named directly; any private individuals named in these records are not.
  • A pattern across four independent states, over 26 years — including six completed default judgments. Court records show: five default judgments in Delaware alone, including a $251,694.69 mortgage-foreclosure default judgment (2009, naming Baber alongside a co-defendant, entered after he was not personally served and did not respond) and a $4,811.29 landlord judgment with possession awarded (2001); a 1998 Maryland traffic case (Allegany County) where the docket records a "Failure to Appear" and a same-day driving-privilege suspension; a 2001 New Jersey disorderly conduct case (Somerdale Boro Municipal Court) where a bench warrant, issued after the case went delinquent, remained outstanding for eighteen years before being recalled in November 2019; a 2009 New Jersey cell-phone-while-driving citation (Carneys Point-Pennsville) with a warrant that shows as still outstanding, with no recall date, as of the record obtained; a 2023 Pennsylvania civil case in which Baber's property management company, Reliant Realty Property Management, obtained a default judgment of $2,659.81 against him after he did not respond or appear, later certified toward collection before being vacated for reasons the docket does not state; and a Delaware debt case (Robin Drive Auto, LLC) still open more than two years after filing, following repeated failed service attempts, now proceeding via Delaware's long-arm statute.[30]
  • Not part of that pattern: A Delaware landlord case (Cranston Hall Apartments, 2024) was resolved by a negotiated settlement rather than default, and other Delaware and Tennessee cases were dismissed or closed simply because the plaintiff could never locate/serve Baber — an unsuccessful service attempt on its own isn't strong evidence of deliberate evasion. These are different kinds of outcomes than the default-judgment pattern above and shouldn't be read as evasiveness.[31]
  • 1994–95 Illinois record (Williamson County). Six misdemeanor/traffic cases from Baber's late teens/early twenties: two retail-theft charges (one resulted in a guilty plea and court supervision that was later satisfied; the other was dismissed on the State's own motion), two uninsured-vehicle citations resolved by guilty plea and paid fines, and two speeding tickets, one of which triggered a bond-forfeiture notice before being resolved the same day as a guilty plea and paid fine. No default judgments appear in this batch, and most matters were resolved and paid.[32]
  • Tennessee debt-collection filings (Montgomery County). Four civil cases from two medical/EMS providers and a consumer lender show "Open" status with no judgment ever entered; in three of the four, court records show the process server was unable to locate or serve Baber at all ("Returned – Not Served"), so these matters do not appear to have proceeded past initial filing.[33]
  • Pennsylvania DA complaint. Separately from the civil judgments above, Baber has, in his own written communications, discussed a complaint made against his "cold case consultant" business to the Pennsylvania DA's office and a related document request from an FBI field office. That a complaint was made and discussed by Baber is documented; the outcome or current status of any resulting inquiry is not established in the material reviewed, and a complaint or inquiry is not evidence of wrongdoing.[34]

6. Source testimony and corroboration ↑ Back to top

A substantial portion of the personal-conduct claims examined in the series come from individual sources rather than public records. In a July 15, 2026 exchange, Baber himself acknowledged the volume of this testimony, while disputing its accuracy: Oranchak told him the claims came from "dozens of people... many of whom never met each other but tell very similar stories," and that this pattern of independent, unprompted convergence — not any single source — is what the reporting relies on.[35]

Consistent with standard sourcing practice, this page and the underlying videos distinguish between:

  • Named, on-the-record sources (e.g., Steve Hodel, DeWayne Hendrix, Nicholas Burston), whose statements are attributed to them by name;
  • Sources granted anonymity (former employers, associates, family contacts), whose claims are described by number of independent corroborating accounts rather than by identity, consistent with standard practice for protecting sources describing personal or workplace conduct;
  • Documentary evidence (licensing records, email correspondence, video footage), cited independently of any single source's credibility.

Baber was given the opportunity to respond to each specific allegation prior to publication and did so point-by-point; his responses are represented throughout this page and in full on his Substack.[36]

7. Relevance to prospective media and production partners ↑ Back to top

Several outlets and production entities that previously engaged with Baber's claims — including People Magazine, Vanity Fair, The Times of London, and DreamEmpire Films — are described in the source material as having stepped back from projects involving him, citing credibility concerns or disputes over the terms of the collaboration.[14][37] Separately, two established cold-case organizations have stated in writing that Baber has no track record with them despite his public "cold case consultant" branding.[23] Any organization evaluating a documentary, news feature, book, or partnership involving Baber's claims should independently verify factual assertions — particularly claims of law-enforcement confirmation, forensic validation, prior institutional affiliation, or family cooperation — before relying on them, given the documented instances above in which such claims did not hold up to direct inquiry.

Evidence behind the Merrill/Margolis identification

He was investigated years ago as a Black Dahlia suspect and as a possible connection to Zodiac suspect Lawrence Kane, based on historical records.
